.. _connect-app:

====================================
Connect an App to Your Atlas Account
====================================

.. default-domain:: mongodb

.. meta::
   :keywords: oauth, third-party apps, delegated access, app connections, consent, connected apps
   :description: Learn how to connect a third-party application to your MongoDB Atlas account through Atlas App Connections, what delegated access allows, and how to review and revoke app access.

.. contents:: On this page
   :local:
   :backlinks: none
   :depth: 2
   :class: singlecol

Overview
--------

You can enable third-party applications to access your
|service-fullname| account. When you connect an application, it
connects to |service| through
:ref:`Atlas App Connections <atlas-app-connections>`, the
|service| OAuth 2.1 platform. Instead of asking you to create and
paste credentials, the application asks you to sign in to |service|
and authorize it to act on your behalf.

This page describes what happens when you connect a third-party
application to your |service| account, including:

- What delegated access means and what "act on your behalf"
  allows.
- How the connection flow works, from choosing |service| to
  returning to the application.
- How to review the permissions you grant on the consent screen.
- Where to view the applications you have connected.
- How to revoke access, and what revocation does and does not do.

.. _connect-app-what-it-does:

What Connecting Your Atlas Account Does
---------------------------------------

When you connect your |service| account to a third-party application:

- **Your data stays in your Atlas account.** Connecting an
  application does not move the source of your data or make that
  application the system of record. Your clusters, data, and other
  |service| resources remain in your own |service| account and
  under your control.
- **The application acts on your behalf through delegated access.**
  |service| issues the application OAuth tokens that let it perform
  |service| actions with your permissions. See
  :ref:`connect-app-delegated-access`.
- **You are billed for the resources the application uses.** Any
  usage of |service| resources that an application provisions or
  operates on your behalf is billed to your own |service| account.

.. _connect-app-delegated-access:

How Delegated Access Works
--------------------------

When you authorize an application, it acts on *your* behalf.
|service| issues the application tokens that let it call the
{+atlas-admin-api+} with the **same permissions you hold**
in your |service| organizations and projects. If you cannot perform
an action in |service|, the application cannot perform it on your
behalf either.

Because access is delegated from your own permissions:

- The application's access changes automatically when your roles
  change. If your |service| access is reduced or removed, the
  application's access updates to match.
- The application can act only in organizations that allow
  third-party app connections. Organization owners control this
  setting. Even after you authorize an application, it cannot
  operate in an organization that has not enabled third-party app
  connections.

Currently, delegated access is not scoped to specific
organizations, projects, or operations: an application can act
anywhere your |service| permissions allow. Because an application
acts with your full |service| permissions, authorize only
applications that you trust.

.. _connect-app-flow:

Connect an App to Atlas
-----------------------

You start the connection from the application, not from |service|.
The exact wording and screens vary by application, but the
|service| portion of the flow is the same for every application.

.. procedure::
   :style: normal

   .. step:: Choose Atlas in the application.

      In the application, choose |service-fullname| as your
      database or start the option to connect your |service|
      account. The application redirects you to |service| to sign
      in.

   .. step:: Sign in to or sign up for Atlas.

      Sign in with your |service| account. If you are already
      signed in, |service| may skip this step. If you do not have
      an |service| account, you can create one to continue.

   .. step:: Review and accept the requested permissions.

      |service| shows a consent screen that lists the permissions
      the application is requesting. Review the permissions, then
      select :guilabel:`Authorize` to grant access or
      :guilabel:`Decline` to cancel. For details about what the
      consent screen shows, see
      :ref:`connect-app-consent`.

   .. step:: Return to the application.

      After you select :guilabel:`Authorize`, |service| returns you
      to the application, which completes the connection.
      The application can now act on your behalf with your
      |service| permissions.

.. _connect-app-consent:

Review Permissions and Consent
------------------------------

Before you authorize an application, |service| shows a consent
screen that summarizes the access the application is requesting:
the ability to see which |service| resources you can access and to
act on your behalf, with the same permissions you hold, in
organizations that allow third-party app connections.

If you select :guilabel:`Authorize`, |service| grants the
application delegated access and returns you to the application. If
you select :guilabel:`Decline`, |service| cancels the connection
and the application does not receive access.

.. _connect-app-view-connected:

View Your Connected Apps
------------------------

You can review the applications you have authorized to act on your
behalf from your |service| account settings. In
:guilabel:`Atlas Settings`, select :guilabel:`App Connections`.

Organization owners view all applications authorized in an
organization from :guilabel:`Organization Settings`; see
:ref:`app-connections-view-authorized-apps`.

.. _connect-app-revoke:

Revoke Access
-------------

You can revoke an application's access at any time from the same
place where you view your connected apps.

.. procedure::
   :style: normal

   .. step:: Go to your App Connections settings.

      In :guilabel:`Atlas Settings`, select
      :guilabel:`App Connections`.

   .. step:: Revoke the application's access.

      Find the application, then select :guilabel:`Revoke`.
      |service| asks you to confirm. Select :guilabel:`Revoke` to
      continue, or :guilabel:`Cancel` to keep the connection.

After you revoke access, the application can no longer act on your
behalf in any organization, and must be re-authorized before it can
connect again. Changes can take up to 10 minutes to take effect.

Organization owners can also disable third-party app connections
for the whole organization; see :ref:`app-connections-enable-disable`.

What Revocation Does and Doesn't Do
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

When you revoke access, the application can no longer take actions
on your behalf.

.. important::

   Revoking access does not delete the resources the application
   created for you, such as database users, clusters, or projects.
   These resources, and any credentials the application created,
   remain active until you or your organization owner remove them.

If you no longer use an application, ask your organization owner to
review and remove any resources it created. For organization-level
management and offboarding steps, see
:ref:`manage-app-access`.
