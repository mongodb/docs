================================
Export Logs to External Services
================================

.. meta::
   :description: Configure your M10+ Atlas clusters to export system logs every minute to external services, including AWS S3, Azure Blob Storage, Datadog, Google Cloud Storage, and Splunk.

.. default-domain:: mongodb

.. facet::
   :name: genre
   :values: reference

.. contents:: On this page
   :local:
   :backlinks: none
   :depth: 1
   :class: singlecol

You can configure your ``M10+`` {+service+} clusters to export
system logs every minute to an external service. Exporting logs to
an external service enables centralized log management, long-term
retention, and integration with your existing monitoring and
observability tools.

Supported Log Types
-------------------

{+service+} supports exporting the following log types:

- ``mongod`` logs
- ``mongos`` logs
- ``mongod-audit`` logs
- ``mongos-audit`` logs

You can specify which log types you want to export and configure
up to 10 export paths to send logs to multiple destinations
simultaneously.

.. important::

   Logs can contain sensitive information (including PII). You are
   responsible for the storage and treatment of your logs in your
   external service. To have {+service+}
   :ref:`redact certain information <database-log-redaction>` before
   exporting logs, contact |mdb-support|.

Considerations
--------------

- You must have an ``M10+`` cluster to export logs. Log export is
  not available for free or shared-tier clusters.
- You must have :authrole:`Project Owner` or
  :authrole:`Organization Owner` access to configure log export.
- Each {+service+} host typically produces 1 GB of logs per day.
  Exporting logs incurs a data transfer cost. The exact data
  transfer cost varies depending on the destination, region, and
  cloud provider.
- Log egress originates from the |service| control plane in
  |aws| ``us-east-1``, regardless of your cluster's region
  or cloud provider. If your cluster is not in |aws|
  ``us-east-1``, you incur cross-region or inter-cloud data
  transfer charges in addition to internet egress costs. To
  learn more about data transfer costs, see
  :ref:`data-transfer-costs`.
- Network issues or retries can cause duplicate log entries in
  your external service.

Failure Handling and Automatic Recovery
----------------------------------------

{+service+} automatically handles log export failures and recovers
without manual intervention:

Retry and Circuit Breaker
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

When log delivery to your external sink fails, {+service+}
automatically retries with exponential backoff for up to 3 attempts by
default. The retry delay starts at 1 second and doubles up to a
maximum of 10 seconds between attempts.

After 5 consecutive failures, a circuit breaker opens to prevent
hammering your sink with repeated requests. The circuit breaker stays
open for 5 minutes before it attempts a test write. If the test
succeeds, normal operation resumes. If it fails, the circuit breaker
reopens.

Each sink configuration has its own independent circuit breaker, so
issues with one integration don't affect others.

Dead Letter Queue (DLQ)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

When retries are exhausted or errors are non-retryable (such as
invalid credentials), {+service+} queues failed log events in a Dead
Letter Queue (DLQ) with a 7-day retention. The DLQ prevents data loss
while your sink is unavailable.

.. warning::

   Logs older than 7 days are permanently deleted from the DLQ. If
   your sink remains inaccessible for more than 7 days, those logs
   cannot be recovered.

When connectivity is restored and the circuit breaker closes,
{+service+} automatically replays queued logs from the DLQ to your
sink. No manual intervention is required.

.. warning::

   If you modify the sink configuration while logs are queued in the
   DLQ, the queued logs will use the new configuration when replayed.
   For example, if you change the destination from one S3 bucket to
   another, queued logs will be sent to the new bucket rather than the
   original destination.

Monitoring and Alerts
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

{+service+} provides :ref:`default alerts <default-alert-settings>`
that automatically send email notifications to :authrole:`Project Owner`
users when:

- Log export encounters non-retryable errors (logs queued in DLQ).
- Log export fails after multiple retry attempts (logs queued in DLQ).
- Log export replay fails to deliver records.

Additionally, the :ref:`Project Activity Feed <activity-feed>` logs
the following informational events:

- Log export recovered (queued logs ready to resume).
- Log export recovered (queued logs now being sent).
- All queued logs delivered.

You can customize alert recipients and notification methods in your
project's alert settings. For details, see
:ref:`configure-alert-settings`.

Supported Export Destinations
-----------------------------

The following table summarizes the external services that
{+service+} supports for log export:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 70

   * - Destination
     - Description

   * - :ref:`AWS S3 <export-logs-external-sinks>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to an |aws|
       |s3| bucket. Supports Multi-Region Access Points (MRAPs).

   * - :ref:`Azure Blob Storage <export-logs-azure>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to an
       {+az-bs+} container.

   * - :ref:`Datadog <export-logs-datadog>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to Datadog
       for real-time log analysis and dashboards.

   * - :ref:`Google Cloud Storage <export-logs-gcs>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to a
       {+gcs+} bucket.

   * - :ref:`OpenTelemetry <export-logs-otel>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to an
       OpenTelemetry (OTel) endpoint for vendor-neutral
       observability.

   * - :ref:`Splunk <export-logs-splunk>`
     - Export ``mongod``, ``mongos``, and audit logs to Splunk
       Cloud or Splunk Enterprise for centralized log search
       and analysis.
